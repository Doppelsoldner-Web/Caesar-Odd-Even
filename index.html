How do I use this?

A short tour of the encoder and the decoder: what each control does, and how to carry a message from one to the other.

encode.js decode.js

Encoding guide

  1. Write your phrase. Type or paste it into Original phrase. Tick Preserve special characters and accents to keep digits, punctuation and accents as typed (only A–Z and a–z get ciphered). Leave it off and accents become plain letters while digits and symbols are dropped.
  2. Click Configure shifts. Every word gets its own row with two boxes: one shift for its odd letters (1st, 3rd, 5th…) and one for its even letters (2nd, 4th, 6th…).
  3. Set the shifts. A shift is how many places a letter moves forward in the alphabet, from 0 to 25, wrapping around after Z. Type a number, or drag the thin track under any box. In the live preview, each original letter sits above the letter it becomes.
  4. Click Encode. You get the encoded message, the word lengths and the shifts used. Keep all three: the decoder needs them.

How it works. Each word is ciphered on its own, then the words are interleaved: the first letter of every word, then the second letter of every word, and so on, as one block without spaces. That is why decoding also needs the word lengths.

Advanced functions

Click Configure shifts and a Quick Fill / Advanced Patterns panel appears under the word rows, so you never have to type every shift by hand.

Odd Pattern Even Pattern Apply
Type one number (2) to use it for every word, or a comma-separated list (1,4) to cycle through the words in order, then press Apply. Apply only fills boxes that are empty or 0, so shifts you already set are kept.
🎲
Random. Fills every odd and even box with a random number from 0 to 25.
Reset Shifts
Sets every box back to 0.
ROT13 Fibonacci Progressive (+1,+2)
Presets: one click fills every box, replacing what is there. ROT13 puts 13 everywhere. Fibonacci walks 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 across the words, with even letters one step ahead of odd ones. Progressive starts at 1 for odd letters and 2 for even letters, then adds one with each word.
Cipher Strength
The bar under the live preview rates how varied your shifts are. The more different numbers across all the boxes, the fuller it gets: Weak, Medium, Strong. One number everywhere, like ROT13, rates Weak. It is a quick guide, not real security.

Export and copy

After Encode the result shows up in three boxes: the encoded message, the word lengths and the shifts used. There are three ways to take it with you.

  • One piece at a time

    Every box has a small copy button. It reads copied! for a moment when it worked. Handy when you only need one piece.

  • Everything at once

    Copy All Data puts all three on your clipboard as one labelled block (Message, Lengths, Shifts). The decoder's Paste Data 📋 button reads exactly that.

  • As a link

    Copy link 🔗 copies an address that re-opens the message and the word lengths in the Decoder, and the shifts too while include shifts in link is ticked. Untick it to send the shifts some other way. The link also appears in a field under the button.

Want to test the round trip? Transfer to Decoder ⇄ sends the message and the lengths to the decode.js tab. You add the shifts there.

Decoding guide

Decoding runs the encoder backwards. It needs the same three things the encoder gave you: the encoded message, the word lengths and the shifts (an odd and an even number for every word).

  1. Fill in the message and the lengths. Use any of the four methods below. A shared link or Paste Data 📋 also builds the shift rows and, when the shifts are included, fills them in for you.
  2. Click Configure shifts. One row appears for each word in the lengths. Skip this step if a link or Paste Data already did it.
  3. Check the shifts. Every word needs its odd and its even shift, from 0 to 25, exactly as used when encoding. The live preview shows each letter turning back into the original.
  4. Click Decode. The original phrase appears in the console, with its own copy button.

If something looks off. The message must have exactly as many characters as the word lengths add up to. Gibberish means a shift or a length differs from the one used to encode. If you edit the lengths after configuring, the shift rows fade: click Configure shifts again.

Four ways to fill in the decoder

Pick whichever fits how the message reached you.

  1. Open a shared link

    Open a link made with Copy link 🔗. The page jumps straight to decode.js and fills in the message and the word lengths, plus the shifts if the link carried them. Then press Decode. If the shifts were left out, type them in first.

  2. Use Paste Data 📋

    In the encoder, click Copy All Data. Then, in the decoder, click Paste Data 📋: it fills in the message and the lengths, builds the rows and applies the shifts in one go. Your browser may ask permission to read the clipboard.

  3. Paste block by block

    Copy each box with its small copy button. Paste the encoded result into Encoded message and the word lengths into Original word lengths, then click Configure shifts. Enter the pairs from the shifts box in the rows: each [odd, even] pair belongs to the row with the same number.

  4. Type everything by hand

    Type the encoded message, then the word lengths separated by commas (like 5, 2, 3, 5), click Configure shifts and fill in each row. Press Enter, or type two digits, to jump to the next box.
